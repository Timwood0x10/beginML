# The Math Behind Self-Attention, Explained

**Core question: why is the dimension of the input vector matrix 64 in self-attention, while the embedding vectors and the encoder input/output vectors have dimension 512?**

### 🌟 One-Sentence Summary:
> **512 is the `information channel width` of the whole model (the embedding dimension), while 64 is the `information-processing width inside one attention head`**.  
> Multiple 64-dimensional heads work in parallel and together add up to exactly 512 — this guarantees expressive power while also improving parallelism and diversity.

---

## 1. First, Clarify What These Dimensions Mean

Let's use the standard Transformer's **Encoder Self-Attention layer** as an example:

| Vector type | Dimension | Explanation |
|--------|------|------|
| **Word Embedding (Input Embedding)** | 512 | Each word is mapped to a 512-dimensional vector |
| **Weight matrices** $W^Q, W^K, W^V$ | $512 \times 64$ | Projection matrices that map the input into subspaces |
| **Feature matrices (Q, K, V)** | $n \times 64$ | The feature vector for each Token in the sequence ($n$ is the sequence length) | 
| **Multi-head attention output** | 512 | After concatenating all heads' outputs, linearly transformed back to 512 dimensions |

---

## 2. Why 512? — The Model's Overall `Expressive Power` Dimension

### ✅ 512 is the **model's hidden layer dimension (hidden size)**, also called $d_{model}$
- This number determines how much information the model can `remember`.
- In a Transformer, all inputs, outputs, residual connections, and feed-forward networks are based on this dimension.
- Why 512? **It's an engineering trade-off:**
  - Too small (e.g., 128): model capacity is insufficient, weak expressiveness.
  - Too large (e.g., 2048): computation explodes, training is slow, prone to overfitting.
  - 512 was, given the hardware of the time (2017), **the sweet spot between performance and efficiency**.

> 💡 Analogy: 512 is like the `number of lanes on a highway` — too narrow and it jams, too wide and it's wasteful.

---

## 3. Why 64? — The `Attention Granularity` of a Single Head

### ✅ 64 is the **dimension of each attention head**, also called $d_k$ (the Key's dimension) or $d_v$ (the Value's dimension)

In multi-head attention:
- Total dimension $d_{model} = 512$
- Number of heads $h = 8$
- So each head gets: $d_k = d_v = d_{model} / h = 512 / 8 = 64$

### Why design it this way? Three major benefits:

#### ✅ 1. **Parallelism and diversity (the core advantage)**
- If we used a single 512-dimensional attention head, it could only learn one `attention pattern` (e.g., only paying attention to subjects, or only to verbs).
- But with **8 64-dimensional heads**, each head can **independently learn a different attention pattern**:
  - Head 1: focuses on grammatical structure
  - Head 2: focuses on coreference relationships (e.g., what `he` refers to)
  - Head 3: focuses on sentiment words
  - …
- This is called **`multi-subspace representation`** of attention, greatly improving the model's expressive power.

> 🎯 Analogy: it's like having 8 experts read an article at the same time — each focuses on one aspect, and then you combine their views; that's more comprehensive than one person reading everything.

#### ✅ 2. **Reduced computational complexity**
- Attention has computational complexity $O(n^2 d)$, where $n$ is the sequence length and $d$ is the dimension.
- With a single 512-dimensional head, the computation is heavy.
- With 8 64-dimensional heads, each head costs $O(n^2 \cdot 64)$, and the total is $8 \times O(n^2 \cdot 64) = O(n^2 \cdot 512)$ — **the same as a single head**, but it can be computed **in parallel**, so it's actually faster.

#### ✅ 3. **Preventing softmax gradient vanishing (technical detail)**
- When computing attention weights, we do $\text{softmax}(QK^T / \sqrt{d_k})$.
- If $d_k$ is too large (e.g., 512), the variance of the dot product $QK^T$ becomes large, pushing the softmax output close to one-hot (only the max value has probability, the rest near 0).
- **Gradient vanishing principle**: the derivative of the Softmax function $S(x_i)$ contains the term $S(x_i)(1 - S(x_i))$. When $S(x_i)$ is close to 0 or 1, the gradient approaches 0, causing backpropagation to fail.
- So we scale by $\sqrt{d_k}$, and $d_k = 64$ happens to keep the scaled values' variance stable (experiments found 64 works best).

> **Mathematical derivation of $d_k$**:
> Assume the elements of $Q$ and $K$ are independent random variables with mean 0 and variance 1. 
> $$ q \cdot k  = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i $$
> By probability theory:
> - Expectation: $\mathbb{E}[q \cdot k] = \sum \mathbb{E}[q_i] \mathbb{E}[k_i] = 0$
> - Variance: $\text{Var}[q \cdot k] = \sum \text{Var}[q_i k_i] = \sum (1 \cdot 1) = d_k$
> 
> So the standard deviation of the dot product is $\sqrt{d_k}$. When $d_k = 64$, the standard deviation is 8. If we didn't divide by 8, the dot product could get very large (e.g., 24), and $e^{24}$ is enormous, pushing Softmax into the saturation region.

---

## 4. Mathematical Derivation: How Multi-Head Attention Keeps Dimensions Consistent

The Transformer's multi-head attention formula:

$$
\text{MultiHead}(Q, K, V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_h) W^O
$$

where each head is:

$$
\text{head}_i = \text{Attention}(Q W_i^Q, K W_i^K, V W_i^V)
$$

- $W_i^Q, W_i^K \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{model} \times d_k}$
- $W_i^V \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{model} \times d_v}$
- $W^O \in \mathbb{R}^{h \cdot d_v \times d_{model}}$

Plugging in the numbers:
- $d_{model} = 512$
- $h = 8$
- $d_k = d_v = 64$

So:
1. Each head outputs 64 dimensions
2. Concatenating 8 heads gives $8 \times 64 = 512$ dimensions
3. Then multiply by $W^O \in \mathbb{R}^{512 \times 512}$, the output is still 512 dimensions

✅ **Perfect alignment! Input 512, output 512, ready for the residual connection.**

---

## 5. What If We Didn't Use 64?

| Case | Consequence |
|------|------|
| `d_k` too small (e.g., 16) | Each head is too weak to learn useful patterns |
| `d_k` too large (e.g., 128) | Must reduce the number of heads (e.g., to 4), diversity drops; softmax gradients also become unstable |
| No multi-head, single 512 head | The model can only learn one attention pattern, limited expressiveness |

> 🔬 Experiments show: 8 heads of 64 dimensions outperform 1 head of 512 dimensions.

## 6. The Q, K, V Matrices in Detail

In self-attention, Q, K, V are three vectors (matrices) with different roles, and the core relationship between them is:

$$
\text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V
$$

That is: **Q and K do a dot product to compute `weights`, and those weights then do a weighted average of V**, producing the final output.

### 1. Clarifying Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Correct Explanation |
|------|----------|
| ❌ Q, K, V are equal | ✅ In self-attention: they come from the same input but go through different linear transformations ($W^Q, W^K, W^V$)<br>✅ In cross-attention: Q comes from the decoder, K and V come from the encoder |
| ❌ Q × K = V | ✅ No such equation! The correct one is $\text{softmax}(QK^T) \times V$ |
| ❌ $QK^T$ is the matrix multiplication result | ✅ Yes, but it's only the intermediate `relevance score`, not the final output |
| ❌ V is the output | ✅ V is the candidate information pool; the final output is a weighted sum of V |

## 7. The FFN Hidden Layer (2048)

Right after the Attention layer comes a Feed-Forward Network (FFN).
Formula:

$$ \text{FFN}(x) = \max(0, xW_1 + b_1)W_2 + b_2 $$

- $W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{512 \times 2048}$: expands the dimension 4x, mapping features into a high-dimensional space.
- $W_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{2048 \times 512}$: shrinks the dimension 4x, compressing features back to the channel width.

Attention is responsible for **`extracting relationships` (who looks at whom)**, while the FFN is responsible for **`processing information` (understanding meaning)**. The FFN's dimension expansion is like the brain recruiting more neurons to process complex concepts (by Cover's theorem, higher-dimensional spaces are more linearly separable).

---

## 8. Positional Encoding

Self-attention has a fatal mathematical flaw: it is `permutation invariant`.
$$ \text{Attention}(A,B) = \text{Attention}(B,A) $$

If you shuffle the order of words in a sentence, the $QK^T$ computed by Attention is the same. So we must add **`positional encoding`**.
The sinusoidal positional encoding formula proposed by Vaswani et al.:

$$
PE_{(pos, 2i)} = \sin\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{model}}}\right) \\
PE_{(pos, 2i+1)} = \cos\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{model}}}\right)
$$

It is **added (Add)** directly onto the input Embedding, not concatenated. This exploits the orthogonality of vectors in high-dimensional space (when the dimension is high enough, even mixed together, they can still be told apart).
*Note: the wavelength varies geometrically from $2\pi$ to $10000 \cdot 2\pi$, making it easier for the model to learn relative position relationships.*

---

## 9. The Mathematical Implementation of Mask

In models like the Decoder or GPT, we must prevent `seeing the future`. Mathematically this is done by adding a Mask matrix $M$ to the $QK^T$ matrix:

$$
M_{ij}= \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } i \geq j \\ -\infty & \text{if } i < j \end{cases}
$$

Then compute $QK^T + M$ and apply softmax:

$$
\text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right)
$$

Because $e^{-\infty} = 0$, this forcibly zeroes out the attention weights of `future` positions.

## 10. Residual Connections and Layer Normalization (Add & Norm)

To let the network stack very deep (e.g., GPT-3 has 96 layers), we must solve gradient vanishing and oscillation.

### 1. Mathematical formula
$$ x_{out} = \text{LayerNorm}(x_{in} + \text{Sublayer}(x_{in})) $$

where $\text{Sublayer}$ can be Attention or FFN.

### 2. Post-LN vs Pre-LN
- **Post-LN (original Transformer)**: add the residual first, then normalize.
  $$ y = \text{LayerNorm}(x + F(x)) $$
- **Pre-LN (modern LLMs like Llama)**: normalize first, then enter the sublayer, finally add the residual.
  $$ y = x + F(\text{LayerNorm}(x)) $$
  *Pre-LN trains more stably and converges even without warmup.*

---

## Summary

- **Dimension flow**: $512 \to (8 \times 64) \to 512 \to 2048 \to 512$
- **Attention computation**: project Q, K, V $\to$ scaled dot product $\to$ Softmax $\to$ weighted sum
