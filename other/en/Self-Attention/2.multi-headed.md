> As shown in the figure, this is the underlying matrix computation in multi-head attention. My understanding is this: the sentence is embedded into word vectors $X$, then through the operations of a single $Q, K, V$ matrix, we get the frequency of occurrence of this word. Finally, combining them and multiplying by a weight matrix gives us the entire multi-head attention matrix $Z$?

**Process summary**: Embedding $\to$ per-head computation $\to$ concatenation $\to$ output

---

### ❌ Core Misconception Correction

**The error**: <span style="color: red;">Through the operations of a single $Q, K, V$ matrix, we get the frequency of occurrence of this word.</span>

**✅ Correct explanation**:
The $Q, K, V$ operations are **not** for computing word frequency; they compute how much **Attention** one word should pay to every other word in the sentence (including itself).
This is a **weighted sum** process, whose purpose is to **fuse contextual information**.

---

### **Let's walk through the 5 steps in the figure one by one**

#### **Step 1 & 2: Input and Embedding**

- **Understanding**: the sentence is embedded into word vectors $X$.
- **Figure correspondence**: `Thinking Machines` $\to X$
- **First-layer encoder**: the input sentence is converted into the embedding matrix $X$.
- **Later encoders (R in the figure)**: the figure also draws an $R$. This represents that for the 2nd layer and beyond, the input is no longer the raw word embedding $X$, but the previous layer's output $R$.
- **Dimensions**: in the figure's example, $2 \times 4$ (2 words, 4 dimensions). In a standard Transformer, it's usually $n \times 512$.

#### **Step 3: Split into 8 heads**

- **Understanding**: through the operations of a single $Q, K, V$ matrix.
- **Figure correspondence**: $W_0, W_1 \dots W_7 \to Q/K/V$
- **This needs refinement**: there isn't just one $Q, K, V$ matrix. To achieve **multi-head**, the model prepares **8 completely different** sets of weight matrices.
   - $W_0^Q \dots W_7^Q, \quad W_0^K \dots W_7^K, \quad W_0^V \dots W_7^V$
- Then, for each head, multiply the input $X$ by its own dedicated weight matrix to get that head's $Q, K, V$:
    - $Q_i = X \times W_i^Q$
    - $K_i = X \times W_i^K$
    - $V_i = X \times W_i^V$
    - **Result**: 8 different sets of Query, Key, Value matrices.
- **Purpose**: let the 8 heads analyze the sentence from 8 different **subspaces** or **perspectives**.

#### **Step 4: Calculate attention — the core step**

- **Understanding**: <span style="color: red;">get the frequency of occurrence of this word.</span>
- **Figure correspondence**: $Q, K, V \to Z$

- **This is the key correction!** This step computes a **brand-new word vector that has fused contextual information**. Using Head 0 as an example, here's how its $Z_i$ is produced:

    1.  **Compute the relevance score (Score)**:
        Take $Q_i$ and do a dot product with $K_i$ ($Q_i \cdot K_i^T$). Each row of this score matrix represents a word and its **degree of match** with every other word. The higher the score, the tighter the association.
    
    2.  **Normalize (Softmax)**:
        $$ \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_i K_i^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) $$
        Divide the score matrix by $\sqrt{d_k}$.
        *   **Why 8?** Because usually $d_k=64$, so $\sqrt{64}=8$.
        *   **Why divide?** To prevent the dot product from becoming too large and causing Softmax gradients to vanish.
        After the `softmax` function, each row of the matrix sums to 1. This weight matrix $A$ is what **attention** really means — how much attention a word should place on other words.

    3.  **Weighted sum**:
        Multiply the attention weights $A$ from the previous step by the $V_i$ matrix.
        $$ Z_i = A \times V_i $$
    
- **What the result $Z_i$ means**: $Z_i$ is a new word-vector matrix. For the word "Thinking", its new vector in $Z_i$ is the weighted average of the $V_i$ vectors of all words in the sentence ("Thinking" and "Machines"), with the weights being the attention scores just computed. So $Z_i$ is a **new representation that fully absorbs contextual information** — not word frequency.

#### **Step 5: Concatenate and Output**

- **Understanding**: combine them and multiply by a weight matrix to get the entire multi-head attention matrix $Z$.
- **Figure correspondence**: $Z_0, Z_1 \dots Z_7 \to Z$
- **Completely correct!**
    1.  **Concatenate**: concatenate the results $Z_i$ computed by all 8 heads along the feature dimension. If each $Z_i$ is $2 \times 64$, after concatenation we get a $2 \times 512$ matrix.
    2.  **Final projection**: multiply this concatenated matrix by a final weight matrix $W^O$. This fuses the information from all 8 heads and projects it back to the model's main dimension (512), so the next encoder layer can process it. The result is the output matrix $Z$.

    3.  **Purpose**: fuse the observations from 8 different perspectives and compress/reorganize the data back into the standard dimension suitable for the next layer.

### **📚 A Vivid Analogy for Understanding Q, K, V**

Imagine self-attention as looking up information in a library:

- **Query (Q)**: the question in your head, like `the history of neural networks`.
- **Key (K)**: the classification labels on the spine of every book in the library, like `deep learning`, `history`, `cooking`.
- **Attention Score**: you take your question (Q) and scan all the labels (K), computing the match:
    * 90% match with `deep learning`
    * 50% match with `history`
    * 0% match with `cooking`
- **Value (V)**: the actual content inside the books.
- **Output (Z)**: the report you write. Your report isn't a copy of any single book; it's a digest assembled according to match degree:
    * 90% of the content is excerpted from *Deep Learning* (V)
    * 10% of the content is excerpted from *History* (V)
    * 0% of the content comes from *Cooking* (V)
- **Summary**: the attention mechanism's output $Z$ is a **comprehensive research report**, not a **keyword count**.

**So, the output of the attention mechanism is a brand-new, context-aware representation that fuses all relevant information — not a simple count or frequency.**

### **➕ Supplement: Toy Example (minimal hand computation)**

To fully understand Step 4, assume the sentence has only two words "I am", with dimension 2:

1.  **Input $X$**:
    $$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \text{ (I, am)} $$
2.  **Assume $Q=K=V=X$** (identity matrix weights).
3.  **Compute Score ($QK^T$)**: the result is the identity matrix (each word looking at itself).
4.  **Softmax**:
    $$ \text{softmax}\left(\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\right) \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.73 & 0.27 \\ 0.27 & 0.73 \end{bmatrix} $$
5.  **Output $Z = A \times V$**:
    *   The new vector of the word "I" becomes: $0.73 \times \text{"I"} + 0.27 \times \text{"am"}$
    *   **Conclusion**: "I" absorbs 27% of the information from "am". That's context fusion!

## 📝 Appendix: Complete Formula Derivation

To fully understand how data flows inside, let's derive the complete matrix computation from input $X$ to output $Z$.

### 0. Notation
*   $n$: Sequence Length
*   $d_{model}$: the model's main dimension (e.g., 512)
*   $h$: number of heads (e.g., 8)
*   $d_k$: each head's dimension ($d_k = d_{model} / h = 64$)

### 1. Linear Projections
For the $i$-th head ($i = 1 \dots h$), we have three independent weight matrices:
$$ W_i^Q, W_i^K, W_i^V \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{model} \times d_k} $$

The input matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_{model}}$ is multiplied by each of them:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_i &= X W_i^Q \quad \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_k} \\
K_i &= X W_i^K \quad \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_k} \\
V_i &= X W_i^V \quad \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_k}
\end{aligned}
$$

### 2. Scaled Dot-Product Attention
This is the core computation of a single head:

$$
\text{head}_i = \text{Attention}(Q_i, K_i, V_i) = \underbrace{\text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_i K_i^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)}_{A_i} V_i
$$

**Dimension change analysis**:
1.  **$Q_i K_i^T$**: $(n \times d_k) \times (d_k \times n) \rightarrow (n \times n)$. This gives the $n \times n$ score matrix.
2.  **Softmax**: normalizes each row; the dimension stays $(n \times n)$. This is the attention weight matrix $A_i$.
