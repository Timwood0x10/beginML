# Neuroevolution & Evolutionary Strategies: Zeroth-Order Optimization Beyond Gradients

--- 
⚠️: Background notes

1. AlphaGo (Lee/Zero): its core algorithms are MCTS (Monte Carlo Tree Search) + RL (reinforcement learning). It still relies heavily on gradient descent (Backpropagation) to update the policy network and value network.
2. AlphaStar (StarCraft AI): its core innovation is PBT (Population Based Training). This is a modern variant of evolutionary strategies. It doesn't just train model parameters — it dynamically evolves hyperparameters during training.
3. Google AutoML / NAS: these systems make heavy use of evolutionary algorithms when designing neural network architectures. Because network structure (how many layers? how big are the kernels?) is discrete and non-differentiable, gradient descent is helpless here.

**Conclusion**: evolutionary algorithms aren't meant to replace gradient descent — they fill the blind spots of gradient descent (non-differentiability, extreme non-convexity, sparse rewards, hyperparameter search).

## 1. The Intuitive Difference: Mountain Climber vs. Paratrooper

### 1.1 Gradient Descent

Imagine you're on a foggy mountain and you want to go downhill.

Strategy: you probe the ground around you to find the steepest direction (computing the gradient $\nabla_\theta L$), then take a step.
Limitations:
If the ground is flat (vanishing gradient), you don't know which way to go.
If there are many small pits (local minima), you'll get trapped.
Prerequisite: the terrain must be smooth (differentiable).

### 1.2 Evolution Strategies

A God's-eye view — you don't need to know the slope.

- **Strategy**
    - **Air-drop**: randomly drop 1000 `paratroopers` around your current position (Gaussian perturbations).
    - **Evaluate**: see which paratroopers land lower (smaller Loss).
    - **Aggregate**: your new position = the weighted average of all paratrooper positions (weights based on their performance).

- **Advantages**
    - Doesn't need smooth terrain (works even when non-differentiable).
    - Doesn't easily get stuck in small pits (it's a population search with a global view).
    - Extremely parallelizable (1000 paratroopers can be computed by 1000 CPU cores simultaneously).

- **Limitations**
    - Extremely sample-inefficient (Sample Inefficiency): SGD looks at the map once and knows which way to go (using derivative information), while ES must drop all 1000 paratroopers and trial-and-error before deciding to take a single step. This is fatal when data acquisition is expensive.
    - Curse of Dimensionality: when the parameter space is huge (e.g., 1 billion parameters), 1000 paratroopers are a drop in the ocean relative to the vast space, making it hard to estimate an accurate gradient direction. ES can't easily train the weights of a large LLM directly, but it's excellent for tuning hyperparameters that only have dozens of dimensions.

## 2. The Mathematical Essence of Evolution Strategies (ES): Zeroth-Order Optimization

Traditional genetic algorithms (GA) simulate biological chromosome Crossover and Mutation, usually for discrete encodings (0/1 bit strings).

Evolution Strategies (ES) in modern deep learning are essentially a **gradient-based Smoothed Gradient Approximation**. We don't compute the true gradient; we estimate it through random sampling.

### 2.1 Smoothing the Objective Function

Suppose we want to maximize a reward function $F(\theta)$. If $F$ is non-differentiable or very rugged, we can optimize its Gaussian-smoothed version $J(\theta)$:

$$
J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(\theta, \sigma^2 I)} [F(\theta + \epsilon)]
$$

- $\theta$: model parameters
- $\sigma$: standard deviation of the Gaussian noise
- $\epsilon$: standard normal noise
- $\mathcal{N}(\theta, \sigma^2 I)$: a Gaussian centered at $\theta$ with variance $\sigma^2$.
- $\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(\theta, \sigma^2 I)} [F(\theta + \epsilon)]$: the expectation of $F(\theta + \epsilon)$ over $\mathcal{N}(\theta, \sigma^2 I)$.


Physical meaning: we no longer care about the reward of one exact point; we care about the average reward in the neighborhood of $\theta$.

### 2.2 The Log-Derivative Trick

To obtain the gradient of $J(\theta)$ w.r.t. $\theta$, $\nabla_\theta J(\theta)$, we can use the log-derivative trick (similar to the derivation of the REINFORCE algorithm).

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta) = \nabla_\theta \int p(\epsilon) F(\theta + \sigma \epsilon) d\epsilon \\
\approx \frac{1}{\sigma} \mathbb E_{\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)} [F(\theta + \epsilon)\cdot \epsilon ]
$$

### 2.3 Monte Carlo Estimation

In engineering implementations, we sample $N$ noise samples $\epsilon_i$:

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta) \approx \frac{1}{\sigma N} \sum_{i=1}^N F(\theta + \epsilon_i) \cdot \epsilon_i
$$

This formula tells us that the update direction of evolution strategies is essentially **`the direction of good performance` minus `the direction of bad performance`**.

- If $\epsilon_i$ performs well, $F(\theta + \epsilon_i)$ is large, so $\epsilon_i$ is weighted more, and we move in the direction of $\epsilon_i$.
- This is a Zeroth-Order optimization, because it only uses function values $F(x)$, not the derivative $\nabla F(x)$.

## 3. Modern Variants of Neuroevolution

### 3.1 OpenAI ES (a simplified version of Natural ES)

OpenAI showed in 2017 that simple ES can match reinforcement learning (RL) on MuJoCo and Atari games.

Algorithm flow:

- Fix the center parameter $\theta_t$
- Generate $n$ noises $\epsilon_i $
- Evaluate $R_i^+ = F(\theta_t + \epsilon_i \sigma)$ and $R_i^- = F(\theta_t - \epsilon_i \sigma)$ (Mirror Sampling, used to reduce variance).
- Update $\theta_t$ 

$$
\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t + \alpha \cdot \frac{1}{n\sigma} \sum_{i=1}^n (R_i^+ - R_i^-) \epsilon_i
$$

This is like an SGD without backpropagation.

### 3.2 PBT (Population Based Training) — DeepMind's Big Weapon

PBT doesn't just evolve weights — it also evolves hyperparameters (Learning Rate, Entropy, Discount Factor).

Logic:
- Train 100 models in parallel (a population), each with different hyperparameters.
- **Exploit**: every so often, poorly performing models copy the parameters (Weights) of the best-performing model.
- **Explore**: after copying, randomly perturb (Mutate) the inherited hyperparameters.

Mathematical meaning:
This is a Bi-level Optimization problem.

- Inner level: SGD optimizes the weights $\theta$
- Outer level: an evolutionary algorithm optimizes the hyperparameters $\lambda$

AlphaStar relies on exactly this mechanism to dynamically adjust its strategy over weeks of training, avoiding the disaster of manual hyperparameter tuning.

PBT's core value:
- Automatic tuning: avoids the disaster of manually searching hyperparameters.
- Dynamic adaptation: the best learning rate early in training may differ from later in training; PBT can adjust dynamically.

### 3.3 CMA-ES: The "Adam" of Evolution Strategies

This is the most powerful, most mathematical method in evolutionary algorithms: Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy.

- OpenAI ES's limitation: its exploration range is a perfect circle (an isotropic Gaussian, $\sigma I$).

- CMA-ES's breakthrough: it learns a covariance matrix
    - This means the exploration range becomes an ellipsoid.
    - It can automatically learn correlations between parameters and explore along the direction of valleys (Valley) instead of blindly banging around.

Analogy:

- OpenAI ES $\approx $ SGD (fixed step size)

- CMA-ES $\approx$ Adam / Newton's Method (adaptive second-order optimization).

## 4. A Geometric Explanation of the Loss Landscape

Why is ES sometimes better than SGD? We can understand this through the geometry of the Loss Landscape.

### 4.1 Escaping Local Minima

- SGD: cares about the local curvature at point $\theta$. If $\theta$ is in a very narrow, deep pit (Sharp Minima), SGD gets trapped, or the gradient explodes.

- ES: cares about the volume average within a radius of $\sigma$ around $\theta$.

    - If a pit is very narrow (narrower than $\sigma$), ES will simply `step over` it, because the expected value of $F(\theta + \epsilon \sigma)$ doesn't drop significantly.

    - ES tends to find Flat Minima, and according to our earlier documents, flat minima mean better generalization.

### 4.2 Gradient Deception

In reinforcement learning, rewards are often sparse or delayed.

Only if you do this step correctly will you get a reward 100 steps later.

At that point the gradient's variance is enormous and the signal-to-noise ratio is extremely low.

ES bypasses the single-step gradient noise by directly evaluating the return of the entire trajectory $F(θ)$.

## 5. Engineering Practice: Hand-Writing a Minimal ES Optimizer

This code shows how to train a simple network using only forward passes, without PyTorch's .backward().

```python
import numpy as np

class SimpleES:
    def __init__(self, num_params, sigma=0.1, alpha=0.01):
        self.theta = np.zeros(num_params) # parameter center
        self.sigma = sigma                # noise std dev (Mutation strength)
        self.alpha = alpha                # learning rate
        
    def get_perturbations(self, population_size):
        """generate a population: half positive perturbations, half negative (Mirror Sampling)"""
        epsilon = np.random.randn(population_size // 2, len(self.theta))
        return np.concatenate([epsilon, -epsilon]) # trick: mirror sampling reduces variance
    
    def step(self, epsilon, rewards):
        """
        core parameter update formula:
        theta = theta + alpha * (1 / (N * sigma)) * sum(Reward * epsilon)
        """
        N = len(rewards)
        
        # normalize rewards (very important! like Batch Norm)
        rewards = (rewards - np.mean(rewards)) / (np.std(rewards) + 1e-8)
        
        # compute the Monte Carlo estimate of the gradient
        # dot(epsilon.T, rewards) is equivalent to sum(R_i * eps_i)
        estimated_gradient = np.dot(epsilon.T, rewards) / (N * self.sigma)
        
        # update parameters
        self.theta += self.alpha * estimated_gradient
        return self.theta

# --- simulate a use case ---
# assume the objective function is F(w) = -w^2 (i.e., we want w close to 0)
target_func = lambda w: -np.sum(w**2)

es = SimpleES(num_params=10)
for epoch in range(50):
    # 1. generate 50 "parallel universe" perturbations
    eps = es.get_perturbations(50)
    
    # 2. evaluate the reward of each universe
    rewards = []
    for e in eps:
        # w_try = theta + sigma * epsilon
        w_try = es.theta + es.sigma * e 
        rewards.append(target_func(w_try))
    
    # 3. evolutionary update
    es.step(eps, np.array(rewards))
    
    if epoch % 10 == 0:
        print(f"Epoch {epoch}: Score {np.mean(rewards):.4f}")
```

## 6. Neural Architecture Search (NAS): Evolution in a Discrete Space

This is the core of Google AutoML. Here the optimization target isn't the weights $\theta$ (real numbers) but the graph topology $G$ (discrete).

### 6.1 Gene Encoding
A network structure is encoded as a `gene` (usually an adjacency matrix of a DAG, or a list of operations).

- Gene fragment: **[Conv3x3, ReLU, MaxPool, SkipConnection]**

### 6.2 Evolutionary Operators

- Mutation:
    - Modify an operation: turn Conv3x3 into Conv5x5.
    - Add layers: insert layer C between layer A and layer B.
    - Change connections: add a ResNet residual connection.
- Crossover:
    - Take the first half of network A and the second half of network B and concatenate them.


### 6.3 Weight Sharing

Early NAS was very slow, because every new architecture had to be trained from scratch.
ENAS (Efficient NAS) introduced the concept of computational graph sharing: every subnetwork is a subgraph of one huge graph (Supergraph). This sped up evolutionary search by 1000x.



### 6.4 LLM Model Merging (The New Frontier of Evolutionary Algorithms)

In the LLM era, retraining a large model is too expensive. A new trend is Model Merging (e.g., fusing Llama-3 and Mistral).

- Problem: how do you determine the fusion ratios? How should each layer's weights be weighted?

- Solution: use an evolutionary algorithm (e.g., CMA-ES) to search for the optimal fusion coefficient matrix $M$ on the validation set.

- Effect: SakanaAI's released "evolutionary fused models" surpassed the original models through parameter mixing alone, without any fine-tuning. This is the ultimate example of evolution strategies operating in parameter space.

## 7. The Ultimate Summary

### 7.1 Algorithm Comparison Map

|Feature|Gradient Descent (SGD/Backprop)|Evolution Strategies (ES/Neuroevolution)|
|---|---|---|
Derivative requirement|Needs the first-order derivative $\nabla_\theta L$|Doesn't need it (Zeroth-Order)
Applicable scenarios|Differentiable, dense rewards, supervised learning|Non-differentiable, sparse rewards, black-box optimization
Computational characteristics|Serial dependency (Layer by Layer)|Highly parallel (Population based)
Memory consumption|High (needs to store backprop gradients)|Low (only needs to store the current parameters)
Communication cost|Low|High (if synchronizing parameters across machines)
Main uses|Training weights (Weights)|Training hyperparameters, searching architectures (AutoML)


### 7.2 Philosophical View: Design vs. Emergence

- SGD is design: we carefully design the Loss Function and tell the model how to move at each step. This is a strongly-supervised logic — efficient, but prone to local optima.

- Evolution is emergence: we only define `what it means to live well` (Fitness), then let randomness and statistical laws take over. This is a weakly-supervised logic — inefficient (low sample utilization), but it often finds solutions humans wouldn't expect (like AlphaGo's divine moves, AlphaStar's bizarre tactics).

The future direction: differentiable evolution. The current trend is to make the evolution process itself differentiable, or to use Meta-Learning to learn the rules of evolution, so the "paratroopers" not only know who survived, but roughly know which direction to jump to survive better.
