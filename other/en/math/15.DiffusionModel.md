# Diffusion Models and Stochastic Differential Equations (SDE): From Thermodynamic Entropy Increase to Generative Intelligence


⚠️ Important technical clarification: AI is no longer a simple function mapping

In previous chapters (SVM, neural networks, kernel regression), the models we built were mostly static mappings: $y=f(x)$.
But in generative AI (Sora, Stable Diffusion), the model is no longer a simple function — it's a **simulator of a physical process**.

1. Not $x \to y$, but $t_o \to t_n$: generation is no longer instantaneous; it's a continuous process evolving over time (a flow).

2. Not fitting data, but fitting a gradient field: the model doesn't learn what the data looks like; it learns the **direction in which data flows** in probability space (the Score Function).

3. Physical essence: a diffusion model essentially **solves an inverse stochastic thermodynamic process** — taking a lump of **disordered** thermal energy (noise), reversing the entropy increase, and reconstructing **ordered** matter (images/videos).


*These notes aim to explain the mathematical foundations of machine learning in an intuitive way, valuing both rigor and practical application.*

## 1. Physical Intuition: Entropy Increase and Time Reversal

### 1.1 Forward Process: Non-Equilibrium Thermodynamics

The physical phenomenon of diffusion: a drop of ink dropped into clear water.

- Physical phenomenon: ink molecules are struck by water molecules (Brownian motion) and gradually diffuse until the whole glass becomes uniformly light blue.

- Information-theory view: entropy is maximized. Structural information is drowned by noise, eventually becoming pure Gaussian noise.

- Mathematical description: this is a simple physical diffusion equation; we know exactly how it happens.

### 1.2 Reverse Process

The task of a generative model: from a glass of uniformly light blue water, recover what the ink looked like the moment it was dropped in.

- Macroscopic physics: this is impossible — it violates the second law of thermodynamics (entropy cannot spontaneously decrease).

- Microscopic physics: if we had a `God's-eye view` and knew the potential field acting on each ink molecule at every moment and position, we could apply a reverse force to each molecule and make time flow backward.

Conclusion: the training process of AI is to make a neural network learn this **force field that reverses time**.

## 2. Mathematical Modeling: Stochastic Differential Equations (SDE), from Discrete to Continuous

Early DDPM (Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models) modeled the process as a discrete Markov chain. But the more elegant and essential description is the continuous-time SDE.

### 2.1 Forward SDE (destroying data)

As time $t$ goes from $0$ to $T$, data $x_0$ gradually becomes noise $x_T$. This process is described by an Itô SDE:

$$
d X_t = \underbrace{f(x_t, t) dt}_{\text{drift term}} + \underbrace{g(t) dW_t}_{\text{diffusion term}}
$$

- $f(x_t,t)$: the drift term, representing the trend of the data over time, usually designed as $- \frac{1}{2} \beta_t x_t$, pulling the data toward the origin (preventing the variance from diverging infinitely).

- $g(t)$: the diffusion term (the coefficient controlling the noise injection strength), usually designed as $\sqrt{\beta_t}$, gradually increasing the noise.

- $W_t$: the Wiener Process or the differential of Brownian motion, with $W_{t + \Delta t} - W_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Delta t)$.

This is essentially Newton's second law in a random environment: **change = deterministic force + random impacts**.

### 2.2 Reverse SDE (generating data)

This is the most core mathematical theorem of diffusion models, proved by Anderson (1982). Any forward SDE has a corresponding reverse SDE, with time flowing backward from $T$ to $0$:

$$
d\bar{x}_t = \underbrace{\left[f(\bar{x}_t, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_{\bar{x}} \log p_t(\bar{x}_t)\right]}_{\text{corrected drift term}}dt + g(t)d\bar{W}_t
$$

- $f(\bar{x}_t,t)$: the original drift (e.g., spreading the ink apart).

- $- g(t)^2 \nabla_{\bar{x}} \log p_t(\bar{x}_t)$: the correction term, representing the trend of data over time.
    - $\nabla_{\bar{x}} \log p_t(\bar{x}_t)$: called the Score Function.

    - It points in the direction where the probability density increases fastest (i.e., toward the data manifold).

    - This term fights against diffusion, forcibly pulling strayed molecules back onto the data manifold.

- $g(t)$: the diffusion term, representing the trend of noise over time, usually designed as $\alpha_t$, gradually increasing the noise (preventing the data from disappearing).

- $\bar{W}_t$: the differential of Brownian motion, with $W_{t + \Delta t} - W_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Delta t)$.


The AI's task: in the forward SDE, $f$ and $g$ are hand-designed (known); the only unknown is the Score Function. We need to train a neural network $s_\theta(x,t)$ to approximate it:

$$
s_\theta(x,t) \approx \nabla_{x_t} \log p_t(x_t)
$$

## 3. Score Matching: What Is the Neural Network Actually Learning?

### 3.1 Why Not Learn the Probability $p(x)$ Directly?

- The normalization constant problem: computing $p(x)$ requires integrating over the entire high-dimensional space (the partition function $Z$), which is computationally infeasible.

- The advantage of Score: Score only needs to know the gradient of $p(x)$ at $x$, not the specific form of $p(x)$. $$ \nabla_{x_t} \log p_t(x_t) = \frac{\nabla p(x)}{p(x)} $$ Computing the log-gradient doesn't need the normalization constant (the derivative of a constant is 0), which cleverly bypasses the computational difficulty.

### 3.2 The Equivalence Between Noise Prediction and Score

In engineering implementations (like DDPM), we usually train the network to predict the noise $\epsilon(x,t)$ rather than directly learn the Score Function $s_\theta(x,t)$. This is actually equivalent to learning the Score.

According to Tweedie's Formula (the denoising formula):


$$
\nabla_{x_t} \log p_t(x_t) = - \frac{\epsilon}{\sigma_t}
$$

where $\epsilon$ is the total noise that turned $x_0$ into $x_t$.

So training the network to predict the noise $\epsilon$ is equivalent to indirectly training the network to learn the Score.


Intuition:

- The network predicting `what noise was added` ($\epsilon(x,t)$) is equivalent to learning the `score function` ($s_\theta(x,t)$)

- The negative noise direction ($- \epsilon(x,t)$) is the `denoising direction`.

- The denoising direction is essentially the gradient direction pointing toward the true data distribution (the Score).


Loss function:

$$
L(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, \epsilon}\left[\left\|\epsilon - \epsilon_{\theta}\left(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon, t\right)\right\|^2\right]
$$

At any time $t$, for the noised image $x_t$, the noise predicted by the network should be as close as possible to the actually added noise $\epsilon$.

This looks like simple regression (predicting noise), but is actually fitting the gradient field of the probability distribution in high-dimensional space.

## 4. Numerical Solution: Euler-Maruyama

With the reverse SDE and a trained Score Network, generation is just solving a differential equation.

### 4.1 Discretization Formula
Discretize the reverse SDE (time step $\Delta t < 0$):

$$
x_{t-1} = x_t - \underbrace{\left[f(x_t, t) - g(t)^2 s_{\theta}(x_t, t)\right]\Delta t}_{\text{deterministic backflow}} + \underbrace{g(t)\sqrt{|\Delta t|} z}_{\text{random perturbation}}
$$

where $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$.

### 4.2 The Evolution of Sampling Algorithms
DDPM / SDE Solver: strictly follows the above SDE, adding noise at every step. High generation quality, but slow (needs 1000 steps).

DDIM / Probability Flow ODE:

- **Physical discovery**: if you remove the random term from the SDE and adjust the drift term, you get an ODE (ordinary differential equation) with the same marginal distributions as the SDE.

- **Advantage**: ODE is deterministic and can be accelerated with advanced solvers (like Runge-Kutta), needing only 20-50 steps.

Consistency Models (the Sora era): directly train the model to learn the integral result of the ODE, attempting single-step generation $x_{\Gamma} \to x_{\mathbb{0}}$

## 5. Langevin Dynamics: Connecting Energy Models

If we view the probability distribution as an energy function $p(x) \propto e^{-E(x)}$ (the Boltzmann distribution), then the Score is the direction of force:

$$
\nabla_{x_t} \log p_t(x_t) = - \nabla_{x_t} E(x_t)
$$

Langevin dynamics sampling:

$$
x_{\text{new}} = x + \underbrace{\eta \nabla_{x} \log p(x)}_{\text{move toward the data center (denoising)}} + \underbrace{\sqrt{2\eta} z}_{\text{random thermal motion (prevents deadlock)}}
$$

This explains the physical picture of diffusion models:

- We're on an energy terrain (Loss Landscape).
- Data points are in valleys (low energy).
- The generation process pushes a ball randomly thrown on a hilltop (noise) down the valley along the slope (Score), while gently shaking it (Langevin noise) to prevent it from getting stuck in a wrong shallow pit.

## 6. The Ultimate Summary

### 6.1 The Grand Unification of Physics and AI

|Physics concept|AI counterpart|
|---|---|
|Molecular thermal motion (entropy increase)|Forward noising process (Forward SDE)|
|Force field / potential gradient|Score Function ($\nabla_{x_t} \log p_t(x_t)$)
|Time reversal|Reverse generation process (Reverse SDE)
|Lowest-energy state|Real data distribution manifold

### 6.2 Why Is the SDE View Superior to DDPM?

- Decoupling: SDE separates the `physical model` (differential equations) from the `numerical solver` (Solver). You can use the same trained model and swap in Euler, Heun, or DPM-Solver at inference to trade off speed and quality.

- Continuity: supports interpolation and editing at arbitrary resolutions.

- Theoretical depth: it directly connects to non-equilibrium thermodynamics, providing mathematical guidance for designing better generative models (such as Flow Matching).

One-sentence summary:

Generative AI is essentially building an inverse dynamical system: by learning the gradient field of a high-dimensional probability space, it fights the second law of thermodynamics and reconstructs order from chaos.
