# Graph Neural Networks and Spectral Graph Theory: Harmonic Analysis in Non-Euclidean Spaces

**When data is no longer neatly arranged (like images/text) but has a topological structure (like social networks/molecules), does convolution still exist?**

This chapter takes you into the world of **non-Euclidean geometry**. We'll see that the classic convolutional neural network (CNN) is just a special case of graph neural networks (GNN) on a regular grid. We'll use **Spectral Graph Theory** to reinvent `convolution`.

---

## 1. The Dilemma: When Translation Invariance Disappears

The core power of CNN comes from the **Translation Invariance of the Kernel**.
*   On an image, the `right` of pixel $(0,0)$ and the `right` of pixel $(10,10)$ are defined the same way.
*   We can slide a $3 \times 3$ filter over the image.

But on a graph $G=(V, E)$:
1.  **No direction**: a node's number of neighbors isn't fixed; there's no `up/down/left/right`.
2.  **No translation**: the topology is warped; a `sliding window` can't be defined.

To do deep learning on graphs, we must find a mathematical operator **independent of the topology**.

---

## 2. The Core Operator: The Graph Laplacian

In physics, the core tool for describing `waves` and `diffusion` is the Laplacian operator $\Delta$. In graph theory, its discrete form is the **Laplacian matrix** $\mathbf{L}$.

### 2.1 Definition
Given an undirected graph's adjacency matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and degree matrix $\mathbf{D}$ (diagonal, $\mathbf{D}_{ii} = \sum_j \mathbf{A}_{ij}$):

$$
\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{D} - \mathbf{A}
$$

### 2.2 Physical Meaning: A Discrete Second Derivative
For a signal on the graph $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ (one scalar per node), applying $\mathbf{L}$ to $\mathbf{x}$ actually computes **the difference between a node and its neighbors**:

$$
(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x})_i = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} (\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j)
$$

This is equivalent to $-\Delta f$ in calculus.
*   **Smoothness measure**: $\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{L} \mathbf{x} = \sum_{(i,j)\in E} (\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j)^2$. This quadratic form measures how violently the signal changes on the graph (Dirichlet Energy).
*   **Thermodynamic view**: it describes how heat (or information) diffuses on a network from high-potential nodes to low-potential nodes.

---

## 3. The Frequency-Domain View: The Graph Fourier Transform (GFT)

Since convolution can't be defined in the spatial domain, we turn to the spectral domain.

### 3.1 What Is a Graph's `Frequency`?
In classical signal processing, the Fourier transform projects a signal onto sine waves $e^{i\omega t}$. Sine waves are eigenfunctions of the Laplacian operator: $\Delta e^{i\omega t} = -\omega^2 e^{i\omega t}$.

Similarly, **a graph's eigenvectors are the graph's `sine waves`**.
Eigendecompose $\mathbf{L}$ (it's a real symmetric matrix, so it can always be orthogonally diagonalized):

$$
\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Lambda} \mathbf{U}^T
$$

*   $\mathbf{U} = [\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_N]$: eigenvectors, i.e., the **graph's Fourier basis**.
*   $\mathbf{\Lambda} = \text{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_N)$: eigenvalues, i.e., the **graph's frequencies**.

### 3.2 The Convolution Theorem
The classical convolution theorem says: **time-domain convolution equals frequency-domain multiplication**.
$$
f * g = \mathcal{F}^{-1} (\mathcal{F}(f) \odot \mathcal{F}(g))
$$

We define convolution $*_G$ on the graph:
$$
\mathbf{x} *_G \mathbf{g} = \mathbf{U} ((\mathbf{U}^T \mathbf{g}) \odot (\mathbf{U}^T \mathbf{x})) = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{G}_\theta \mathbf{U}^T \mathbf{x}
$$

where $\mathbf{G}_\theta = \text{diag}(\theta)$ is the filter we learn in the frequency domain.

---

## 4. Escaping $O(N^3)$: From ChebNet to GCN

The spectral graph convolution above has a fatal flaw: the eigendecomposition $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Lambda} \mathbf{U}^T$ costs $O(N^3)$. For a social network with millions of nodes, that's unacceptable.

### 4.1 Chebyshev Approximation (ChebNet)
To avoid explicitly computing the eigendecomposition, we use matrix polynomials to approximate the frequency-domain filter.
If the filter $g_\theta(\Lambda)$ is a degree-$K$ polynomial of $\Lambda$, then:
$$
g_\theta(\mathbf{L}) \approx \sum_{k=0}^K \theta_k \mathbf{L}^k
$$
This means we only need to compute powers of $\mathbf{L}$, corresponding to aggregating information from $K$-hop neighbors — no eigendecomposition needed at all.

### 4.2 GCN (Graph Convolutional Network)
Kipf & Welling (2017) made an extremely bold simplification: take only $K=1$ (look at first-order neighbors only) and set $\theta_0 = -\theta_1 = \theta$.

The resulting propagation formula is extremely concise:

$$
\mathbf{H}^{(l+1)} = \sigma \left( \tilde{\mathbf{D}}^{-\frac{1}{2}} \tilde{\mathbf{A}} \tilde{\mathbf{D}}^{-\frac{1}{2}} \mathbf{H}^{(l)} \mathbf{W}^{(l)} \right)
$$

*   $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{A} + \mathbf{I}$: adding self-loops, so nodes keep their own information during aggregation.
*   $\tilde{\mathbf{D}}^{-\frac{1}{2}} \tilde{\mathbf{A}} \tilde{\mathbf{D}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}$: this is the **normalization** step, preventing features of high-degree nodes from exploding.

**Physical meaning**: every GCN update is essentially **a weighted average of neighbor features, followed by a linear transformation and nonlinear activation**.

---

## 5. The Grand Unification: GNN and Transformer

Let's look back at the Self-Attention in Transformers:
$$
\text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}
$$

Ignoring Softmax and letting $\mathbf{A}_{att} = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T$, the Transformer's update formula is strikingly similar to GCN:
*   **GCN**: aggregation based on the **fixed** graph structure $\mathbf{A}$ (physical connections).
*   **Transformer**: aggregation based on the **dynamically computed** similarity $\mathbf{A}_{att}$ (semantic associations).

**Conclusion**: a Transformer is essentially a GNN running on a **Fully Connected Graph**, where the edge weights are data-driven.

## 6. Code Implementation: GCN Node Classification

We'll implement a hand-written GCN layer with PyTorch and do node classification on the classic `Zachary's Karate Club` dataset.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.optim as optim
import numpy as np
import networkx as nx
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# ==========================================
# 1. data prep: Zachary's Karate Club
# ==========================================
def load_data():
    # load the built-in karate club graph
    # it's a social network; nodes are members, edges are their social relationships
    # task: predict whether a member belongs to the "Mr. Hi" faction or the "Officer" faction
    G = nx.karate_club_graph()
    
    # get the labels (Ground Truth)
    labels = []
    for i in range(len(G.nodes)):
        club = G.nodes[i]['club']
        labels.append(0 if club == 'Mr. Hi' else 1)
    labels = torch.LongTensor(labels)

    # get the adjacency matrix A
    A = nx.adjacency_matrix(G).todense()
    A = np.array(A, dtype=np.float32)
    
    # feature matrix X
    # in this simple example we have no node features, so we use the identity matrix (One-hot encoding)
    # this means the model must learn purely from the graph structure
    X = torch.eye(A.shape[0], dtype=torch.float32)
    
    return G, A, X, labels

# ==========================================
# 2. mathematical core: the Renormalization Trick
# ==========================================
def preprocess_adjacency(A):
    """
    implements the formula from the GCN paper: D^{-1/2} * (A + I) * D^{-1/2}
    this is the first-order Chebyshev approximation of spectral graph convolution.
    """
    N = A.shape[0]
    
    # 1. add self-loops: A_tilde = A + I
    A_tilde = A + np.eye(N)
    
    # 2. compute the degree matrix D_tilde
    D_tilde_vals = np.sum(A_tilde, axis=1) # sum over rows
    
    # 3. compute D^{-1/2}
    # note: without self-loops, a degree of 0 would cause division by zero; adding I makes degrees at least 1
    D_inv_sqrt_vals = np.power(D_tilde_vals, -0.5)
    D_inv_sqrt = np.diag(D_inv_sqrt_vals)
    
    # 4. symmetric normalization: A_hat = D^{-1/2} @ A_tilde @ D^{-1/2}
    # this keeps features from exploding during propagation (numerical stability)
    A_hat = np.dot(np.dot(D_inv_sqrt, A_tilde), D_inv_sqrt)
    
    return torch.FloatTensor(A_hat)

# ==========================================
# 3. model definition: manually implement a GCN layer
# ==========================================
class GCNLayer(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, in_features, out_features):
        super(GCNLayer, self).__init__()
        # define the learnable weight matrix W
        self.linear = nn.Linear(in_features, out_features, bias=False)

    def forward(self, A_hat, X):
        # formula: H = A_hat * X * W
        # 1. linear transformation: XW (PyTorch's Linear layer automatically handles X @ W.T)
        X_proj = self.linear(X)
        
        # 2. neighbor aggregation (message passing): A_hat * (XW)
        # torch.mm is matrix multiplication
        out = torch.mm(A_hat, X_proj)
        
        return out

class GCN(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, num_features, num_classes):
        super(GCN, self).__init__()
        # two GCN layers
        # the first maps features to a hidden space (e.g., 4 dims)
        self.gcn1 = GCNLayer(num_features, 4)
        # the second maps to 2 dims for visualization, also serving as output logits
        self.gcn2 = GCNLayer(4, 2)
        self.relu = nn.ReLU()

    def forward(self, A_hat, X):
        # first layer: Aggregation -> Transformation -> Activation
        h1 = self.relu(self.gcn1(A_hat, X))
        # second layer: Aggregation -> Transformation (no activation, output logits directly)
        h2 = self.gcn2(A_hat, h1)
        return h2

# ==========================================
# 4. training and visualization flow
# ==========================================
def visualize_embedding(h, color, epoch=None, loss=None):
    plt.figure(figsize=(7, 7))
    h = h.detach().numpy()
    plt.scatter(h[:, 0], h[:, 1], s=140, c=color, cmap="coolwarm")
    plt.title(f'Epoch {epoch}, Loss: {loss:.4f}' if epoch else 'Result')
    plt.show()

def main():
    # 1. prepare the data
    G, A_numpy, X, labels = load_data()
    A_hat = preprocess_adjacency(A_numpy) # compute the core operator
    
    # 2. initialize the model
    model = GCN(num_features=X.shape[1], num_classes=2)
    optimizer = optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=0.01)
    criterion = nn.CrossEntropyLoss()

    # 3. training loop
    print("Start training...")
    for epoch in range(201):
        model.train()
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        
        # forward pass
        output = model(A_hat, X)
        
        # compute the loss (we use full supervision here; real GCNs are often semi-supervised)
        loss = criterion(output, labels)
        
        # backpropagation
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()

        if epoch % 50 == 0:
            print(f'Epoch {epoch:03d} | Loss: {loss.item():.4f}')
            # visualize the current embedding state
            # we only trained on graph structure, with no node feature input
            # see how the model separates the two factions purely from "who knows whom"
            # Visualize every 50 epochs (optional, or just at the end)
            # visualize_embedding(output, labels, epoch, loss.item())

    # 4. visualize the final result
    print("Training complete! Showing the final node distribution...")
    model.eval()
    final_output = model(A_hat, X)
    visualize_embedding(final_output, labels, epoch=200, loss=loss.item())

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

*   Implement the `SpectralGraphConv` layer.
*   Implement the `renormalization_trick`.
*   Visualize: how the nodes' Embeddings in 2D space change before and after training (from chaos to linearly separable).

## 7. Summary

| Concept | Euclidean space (CNN) | Non-Euclidean space (GCN) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Base structure** | Grid | Graph |
| **Invariance** | Translation invariance | Topological invariance (Permutation Invariance) |
| **Convolution definition** | Sliding-window weighted sum | Spectral-domain multiplication / spatial message passing |
| **Core operator** | $3\times3$ convolution kernel | Laplacian matrix $\mathbf{L}$ |
| **Computational complexity** | $O(N)$ (with FFT) | $O(E)$ (with polynomial approximation) |
