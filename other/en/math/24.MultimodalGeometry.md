# Multimodal Geometry

**Abstract**: In the early days of deep learning, we processed images (CNN) and text (RNN/Transformer) separately. Today, multimodal large models require us to stitch together vision, language, and even audio. But the image manifold (a high-dimensional continuous pixel space) and the text manifold (a discrete symbolic space) have completely different topological structures. This chapter explores how to use **spherical geometry** for manifold alignment (CLIP), rigorously derives the mathematical link between InfoNCE and the mutual information lower bound, uses **tensor algebra** to capture nonlinear interactions between modalities, and how **Cross-Attention** acts as a "portal" connecting heterogeneous manifolds in SDE dynamics.

---

## 1. Alignment of Heterogeneous Spaces

Image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ and text $y \in \mathbb{N}^L$ live in completely different spaces. To make them communicate, we first need to map them into a shared Embedding Space.

### 1.1 Geometry on the Hypersphere
Modern multimodal models (like CLIP) usually normalize features onto a unit hypersphere: $||z||_2 = 1$.
*   **Eliminating magnitude**: in high-dimensional space, magnitude usually represents signal strength or sentence length, while direction represents semantics. Normalization focuses optimization on the angle $\theta$.
*   **Metric equivalence**: on the unit sphere, Euclidean distance and cosine similarity are strictly monotonically related:
    $$ ||z_I - z_T||^2 = (z_I - z_T)^T (z_I - z_T) = 2 - 2 \cos(z_I, z_T) $$

### 1.2 InfoNCE: A Variational Lower Bound on Mutual Information
We often say InfoNCE Loss "pulls positive samples together and pushes negative samples apart"; its deep mathematical goal is to maximize the **Mutual Information (MI)** between modalities.

**Theorem**: minimizing InfoNCE Loss is equivalent to maximizing a lower bound of the mutual information $I(X; Y)$:
$$ I(X; Y) \ge \log N - \mathcal{L}_{NCE} $$

**Brief derivation**:
1.  **Mutual information definition**: $I(X; Y) = \mathbb{E}_{p(x,y)} \left[ \log \frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)} \right]$.
2.  **Optimal discriminator**: the scoring function $f(x,y)$ in InfoNCE, at optimality, approximates the pointwise mutual information (PMI): $f^*(x,y) = \log \frac{p(y|x)}{p(y)} + C$.
3.  **Substituting into the loss**:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{NCE}] & = \mathbb{E} \left[ -\log \frac{e^{f(x,y)}}{e^{f(x,y)} + \sum_{j=1}^{N-1} e^{f(x,y_j)}} \right] \\
    & \approx \mathbb{E} \left[ \log \left( 1 + \frac{p(y)}{p(y|x)} (N-1) \right) \right] \\
    & \approx \mathbb{E} \left[ \log \left( \frac{p(y)}{p(y|x)} N \right) \right] \quad (\text{when } N \text{ is large}) \\
    & = \log N - \underbrace{\mathbb{E} \left[ \log \frac{p(y|x)}{p(y)} \right]}_{I(X;Y)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Rearranging: $I(X;Y) \ge \log N - \mathcal{L}_{NCE}$.
    This proves: **the larger the number of negative samples $N$, the tighter this lower bound, and the more mutual information the model learns.**

### 1.3 The Physical Meaning of the Temperature Coefficient $\tau$
The loss function contains $\exp(z_I \cdot z_T / \tau)$ with a temperature coefficient.
*   **Small $\tau$ (low temperature)**: the distribution becomes Peaked. The model focuses only on the hardest-to-distinguish negatives (Hard Negatives), corresponding geometrically to local high curvature.
*   **Large $\tau$ (high temperature)**: the distribution tends toward Uniform. Gradients flow to all negative samples.
*   **Connection (See Ch 23)**: here $\tau$ plays exactly the same role as the entropy regularization coefficient $\epsilon$ in the optimal transport Sinkhorn algorithm — **balancing "exact matching" against "maximum entropy"**.

## 2. The Grassmannian Manifold

When the data isn't a point (like an image) but a set (like a video's frames, or a class in few-shot tasks), we're actually comparing two linear subspaces.

### 2.1 Subspace Representation
The **Grassmannian manifold** $Gr(k, n)$ is the set of all $k$-dimensional linear subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^n$.
A video $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times T}$ can be represented as the subspace $U \in Gr(k, n)$ via SVD decomposition $X = U \Sigma V^T$.

### 2.2 Geodesic Distance: Principal Angles
The distance between two subspaces $U_1, U_2$ is no longer Euclidean distance but the **Principal Angles** $\theta_i$.
$$ \cos \theta_i = \sigma_i(U_1^T U_2) $$
This is the mathematical foundation for aligning the "video manifold" with the "text manifold" in multimodal video understanding.

## 3. Tensor Fusion

Simple concatenation $[v_I; v_T]$ is just a linear operation in the additive group; it can't capture multiplicative interactions between modalities (logical AND/OR/NOT).

### 3.1 Outer Product and Cartesian Product
To capture all-around interactions, we compute the **Outer Product**:
$$ Z = v_I \otimes v_T \in \mathbb{R}^{D_I \times D_T} $$
$$ Z_{ij} = v_{I, i} \cdot v_{T, j} $$
This preserves not only features but also the second-order correlations between features.

### 3.2 Low-Rank Tensor Decomposition
To solve the $D^2$ dimension explosion, we use Tucker decomposition or CP decomposition. In deep learning, this is usually implemented as:
$$ Z \approx (v_I W_I) \odot (v_T W_T) $$
where $\odot$ is the Hadamard product (Element-wise product). Mathematically this is equivalent to reconstructing a high-order tensor in a low-rank space; it's common in VQA (Visual Question Answering) tasks.

## 4. Latent-Space Dynamics and SDE

In generative models (Stable Diffusion), multimodal alignment isn't just computing distances — it's **applying force fields**.

### 4.1 Conditional SDE
Reviewing **Chapter 15 (diffusion models)**, the generation process is the reverse SDE:
$$ dx = [f(x, t) - g^2(t) \nabla_x \log p_t(x)] dt + g(t) dw $$

When text $y$ is introduced for conditioning, using Bayes' formula $p(x|y) \propto p(y|x)p(x)$, the Drift Term is corrected to:
$$ \nabla_x \log p_t(x|y) = \underbrace{\nabla_x \log p_t(x)}_{\text{Unconditional Score}} + \lambda \underbrace{\nabla_x \log p_t(y|x)}_{\text{Classifier Guidance}} $$

*   **$\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$**: keeps the image realistic (the manifold's tangent vector).
*   **$\nabla_x \log p_t(y|x)$**: pulls the image toward the direction of the text description (a guiding force between manifolds).
*   **The role of Cross-Attention**: the Cross-Attention module essentially estimates this conditional guidance term. It computes the correlation between the text $y$ and the current noisy image $x_t$, and projects the text features into gradient updates in image space.

## 5. Code Practice: InfoNCE and Manifold Alignment

### 5.1 PyTorch Implementation of InfoNCE (production-grade code)

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def info_nce_loss(features_a, features_b, temperature=0.1):
    """
    Args:
        features_a: (Batch, Dim) normalized image features
        features_b: (Batch, Dim) normalized text features
        temperature: the temperature coefficient tau, controlling the sharpness of the distribution
    """
    # 1. compute the cosine similarity matrix (Batch, Batch)
    # logits[i][j] is the similarity between image i and text j
    logits = torch.matmul(features_a, features_b.T) / temperature
    
    # 2. construct the labels: the diagonal is positive samples (0, 1, 2, ...)
    batch_size = features_a.shape[0]
    labels = torch.arange(batch_size).to(features_a.device)
    
    # 3. bidirectional Cross Entropy (CLIP uses a symmetric loss)
    # Image -> Text: given an image, find the corresponding text
    loss_a2b = F.cross_entropy(logits, labels)
    # Text -> Image: given text, find the corresponding image
    loss_b2a = F.cross_entropy(logits.T, labels)
    
    return (loss_a2b + loss_b2a) / 2
```

### 5.2 Manifold Alignment on the Sphere

```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import matplotlib.cm as cm

def normalize(x):
    """project the vectors onto the unit sphere (circle)"""
    norm = np.linalg.norm(x, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    return x / (norm + 1e-8)

def infonce_loss_gradient(I, T, tau=0.1):
    """
    compute a simplified InfoNCE gradient
    I, T: (Batch_Size, Dim) normalized features
    simulate pulling T toward I (positive samples) and pushing away other I (negative samples)
    """
    N = I.shape[0]
    # similarity matrix (logits)
    logits = np.dot(I, T.T) / tau  # (N, N)
    
    # Softmax probabilities
    exp_logits = np.exp(logits - np.max(logits, axis=1, keepdims=True))
    probs = exp_logits / np.sum(exp_logits, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    
    # gradient derivation: dL/dI_i = sum_j (p_ij - y_ij) * T_j
    # where y_ij is the identity matrix (only the diagonal is positive)
    targets = np.eye(N)
    grad = np.dot((probs - targets), T) / tau
    
    return grad

# ==========================================
# 1. data generation: two unaligned modalities
# ==========================================
np.random.seed(42)
N = 20 # number of samples
dim = 2

# modality A (image): distributed in the upper-right region
feat_A = np.random.randn(N, dim) + np.array([2, 2])
# modality B (text): distributed in the lower-left region, with a shuffled mapping (assumed initially completely unaligned)
feat_B = np.random.randn(N, dim) + np.array([-2, -2])

# normalize onto the unit circle
feat_A = normalize(feat_A)
feat_B = normalize(feat_B)

# ==========================================
# 2. simulate the training process
# ==========================================
lr = 0.5
tau = 0.2
steps = 50

history_A = [feat_A.copy()]
history_B = [feat_B.copy()]

# here we only move feat_B to adapt to feat_A (simplified demo)
for _ in range(steps):
    grad = infonce_loss_gradient(feat_A, feat_B, tau)
    feat_B = feat_B - lr * grad
    feat_B = normalize(feat_B) # project back onto the sphere
    
    history_B.append(feat_B.copy())

# ==========================================
# 3. visualize
# ==========================================
plt.figure(figsize=(12, 5))
colors = cm.rainbow(np.linspace(0, 1, N))

# --- Plot 1: initial state ---
plt.subplot(1, 2, 1)
circle = plt.Circle((0, 0), 1, color='lightgray', fill=False, linestyle='--')
plt.gca().add_artist(circle)

for i in range(N):
    plt.scatter(history_A[0][i, 0], history_A[0][i, 1], color=colors[i], marker='o', label='Image' if i==0 else "")
    plt.scatter(history_B[0][i, 0], history_B[0][i, 1], color=colors[i], marker='x', label='Text' if i==0 else "")
    # draw the pairing lines
    plt.plot([history_A[0][i, 0], history_B[0][i, 0]], 
             [history_A[0][i, 1], history_B[0][i, 1]], 
             color=colors[i], alpha=0.3)

plt.title("Before Alignment (Random Init)")
plt.xlim(-1.5, 1.5); plt.ylim(-1.5, 1.5)
plt.legend()
plt.axis('equal')

# --- Plot 2: final state ---
plt.subplot(1, 2, 2)
circle = plt.Circle((0, 0), 1, color='lightgray', fill=False, linestyle='--')
plt.gca().add_artist(circle)

final_B = history_B[-1]
for i in range(N):
    plt.scatter(feat_A[i, 0], feat_A[i, 1], color=colors[i], marker='o')
    plt.scatter(final_B[i, 0], final_B[i, 1], color=colors[i], marker='x')
    plt.plot([feat_A[i, 0], final_B[i, 0]], 
             [feat_A[i, 1], final_B[i, 1]], 
             color=colors[i], alpha=0.6)

plt.title(f"After Alignment (Contrastive Loss)\nTemperature $\\tau={tau}$")
plt.xlim(-1.5, 1.5); plt.ylim(-1.5, 1.5)
plt.axis('equal')

plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```

**Reading the figure:**
The figure above shows the manifold alignment experiment on a 2D sphere (circle). **Dots (Image)** represent the feature distribution of the image modality, **crosses (Text)** represent the feature distribution of the text modality. The connecting lines represent the **Ground Truth** (true image-text pairing).

1.  **Left plot: Before Alignment**
    *   **Semantic Gap**: image features concentrate in the upper-right quadrant (0° to 90°), while text features concentrate in the lower-left quadrant (-90° to -180°). This distribution difference simulates the phenomenon that, at initialization of a multimodal model, the feature spaces output by the vision encoder and the text encoder don't overlap at all.
    *   **Chaotic correspondences**: the lines connecting same-color point pairs cross the circle's center. In spherical geometry, this means the angle between paired samples is close to $180^\circ$ (cosine similarity near -1) — the model considers these paired image-text items completely unrelated, even opposite.

2.  **Right plot: After Alignment**
    *   **Topological migration**: after gradient-descent optimization with the InfoNCE Loss, the text crosses undergo dramatic displacement, collectively "migrating" to the upper-right quadrant, overlapping with the image features. This verifies that contrastive learning can forcibly pull heterogeneous manifolds together.
    *   **Fine-grained Alignment**: notice the color correspondences. The red cross moved precisely next to the red dot, and the cyan cross also found its cyan dot.
    *   **Geometric repulsion and the temperature coefficient**: although paired points are close, different-colored pairs still maintain spacing without collapsing into one point. This is exactly the effect of the denominator term (negatives) in the **InfoNCE Loss** and the temperature coefficient $\tau=0.2$ — while pulling positives together, it uses the finite volume of the sphere to force negatives to repel each other, preserving the data's **discriminative structure**.

**Conclusion**:
This figure intuitively shows the core mathematical principle of models like CLIP: **learning is a process of rotating, scaling, and aligning the manifolds of different modalities on a hypersphere**. Once alignment is complete, we can simply compute cosine distance (angles) to do cross-modal retrieval or zero-shot classification.

## 6. Summary: A Bridge to AGI

Multimodal geometry is the key connecting Perception and Cognition.

- Spherical geometry (CLIP) solves the **what** problem, establishing a dictionary between modalities.

- Tensor geometry (Tensor Fusion) solves the **how** problem, capturing complex logical interactions.

- Differential geometry (Cross-Attention/Diffusion) solves the **creation** problem, letting semantics guide generation.
